## A. Masking / forward process

**Plan:** mask a percentage of certain features per step until all masked; reverse unmasks n% per step, possibly at a different rate per feature.

- The absorbing-state ("mask") forward kernel is `Q_t = (1 − β_t)I + β_t·1·e_m^T`, written out entrywise as eq (7): stay put with probability 1 − β_t, jump to `[MASK]` with β_t, and `[MASK]` is absorbing; the cumulative kernel is closed-form, `Q̄_t = ᾱ_t I + (1 − ᾱ_t)1e_m^T` with `ᾱ_t = Π(1 − β_i)`. — Austin et al., D3PM (discrete denoising diffusion probabilistic model), §3.1 + App. A.2.2 eq (7) + App. A.4.1 [1] 🟢
- D3PM App. A.3 proves the generative masked-language-model objective (sample a mask count k, mask k tokens, cross-entropy on masked positions) *is* the absorbing-state ELBO (evidence lower bound) up to a per-k reweighting (eqs 11–14); the mutual-information schedule collapses to β_t = 1/(T − t + 1). — Austin et al. [1] 🟢
- MDLM (masked diffusion language model) writes the continuous-time NELBO as `∫ (α′_t/(1−α_t)) Σ_ℓ log⟨x_θ(z_t,t), x^ℓ⟩ dt`, eq (11): "our objective has a simple form: it is the weighted average of masked language modeling (MLM) losses". Training is exactly: sample t ~ U[0,1], mask each token independently with probability 1 − α_t (Alg. 1). — Sahoo et al., §3.5 + Alg. 1 [2] 🟢
- The ELBO is **invariant to the functional form of α_t** (§3.4, change of variables γ = log(1 − α_t)); Table 9 measures log-linear / cosine / cosine² / linear at an identical 3.30 bits-per-dimension on OpenWebText, differing only in per-datapoint variance (1.81 / 3.30 / 3.30 / 7.57). — Sahoo et al., §3.4, App. E.1, Table 9 [2] 🟢
- "Time-conditioning is unnecessary": MDLM omits t from x_θ, which "simplifies sampling, yielding a 2x inference speed-up" (§3.2.2, App. E.2); MD4 shows the time-dependent network implicitly parameterises a time-independent one, so you may condition on the masked *ratio* instead (Prop. 3.1–3.2 in [8]). — Sahoo et al. [2] 🟢; Zheng et al. [8] 🟢
- The closest published relative of "each feature masked at its own rate" is MD4's **state-dependent masking schedule** (GenMD4): a vector schedule α_{t,i}, one entry per *token value* i, forward kernel `Q̄(s,t) = diag(α_t/α_s) + (I − diag(α_t/α_s))1e_m^T`, ELBO eq (12), parameterised as α_{t,i} = 1 − t^{w_i} with learnable w_i > 0. Because q(x_t|x_0) depends on w, the schedule gradient is not reparameterisable and needs a REINFORCE leave-one-out estimator; naive autodiff is biased and pushes w to zero. — Shi et al., MD4 §6 + App. I.1–I.2 [3] 🟢
- What it buys, per the authors: better test likelihood only (text8 1.37 → 1.34 bits-per-character, GenMD4 vs MD4, as tabulated by [5] Table 2), with explicit costs: "GenMD4 is promising, but it can easily overfit to the dataset, making it less effective for zero-shot transfer… inference with a state-dependent schedule is more challenging". — Shi et al., §8 [3] 🟢, cross-checked in Schiff et al. Table 2 [5] 🟢
- The schedule *does* matter for parallel sampling quality even though the ELBO ignores it: MD4 Fig. 2 shows cosine schedule ≈ linear schedule + cosine discretisation grid, both far better in FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) than linear + uniform grid. Cosine "slows down the unmasking at the beginning… a lower chance of conflicting tokens being unmasked simultaneously". — Shi et al., §2 (Masking schedules) + Fig. 2 [3] 🟢
- LLaDA masks with a single ratio t ~ U[0,1] per sequence, each token independently, loss = (1/t)·cross-entropy on masked positions only (eq 3, App. A.1 eqs 7–8); linearity is justified because "the information in the text is proportional to the number of tokens". Structured/block masking appears in LLaDA only at **decoding** (semi-autoregressive block sampling), never as a per-field training rate. — Nie et al., §2.1–2.2, App. A.1, App. B.4 [4] 🟢

**Implication for the repo:** per-field masking rates are not a published training variant — MD4's generalisation is per token *value* and learned by REINFORCE, and the ELBO is schedule-invariant, so hand-tuning a rate per slot/feature buys nothing in likelihood; it only reshapes the unmask-order distribution, which is cheaper and better attacked at decode time (§E).

## B. Classifier-free guidance on a discrete space conditioned on a scalar

**Plan:** condition on a binned win rate, apply classifier-free guidance (CFG), ask for the top bin at inference.

- Original CFG: jointly train conditional and unconditional models by replacing the label with a null token ∅ with probability `p_uncond`, then sample with `ε̃ = (1 + w)ε_θ(z,c) − w·ε_θ(z)` (Alg. 1, Alg. 2, eq 6). p_uncond ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.5} tested; 0.5 is consistently worse, 0.1 and 0.2 tie. — Ho & Salimans, §3.2, §4.2 [6] 🟢
- Guidance strength is a monotone fidelity/diversity dial, not a free win: ImageNet 64×64 FID 1.55 at w = 0.1 degrades to 26.22 at w = 4.0 while Inception Score rises 66 → 260 (Table 1). "Any guidance method that increases sample fidelity at the expense of diversity must face the question of whether decreased diversity is acceptable." — Ho & Salimans, Table 1, §5 [6] 🟢
- Discrete Guidance (verified venue: "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2025") gives the exact discrete analogue on continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs): predictor-guided rates `R_t^(γ)(x,x̃|y) = [p(y|x̃,t)/p(y|x,t)]^γ · R_t(x,x̃)` (eq 2 / eq 21) and predictor-**free** rates `R_t^(γ)(x,x̃|y) = R_t(x,x̃|y)^γ · R_t(x,x̃)^(1−γ)` (eq 3 / eq 26). Tractability comes from only one dimension changing per infinitesimal step, leaving D·(S−1)+1 non-zero rates. — Nisonoff, Xiong, Allenspach, Listgarten, §4 [7] 🟢
- Their own validity finding: "Increasing the guidance strength, γ, led to a decrease in SMILES string validity and we selected γ = 2 for our experiments as the largest γ resulting in a reasonable validity." — Nisonoff et al., §6.1 [7] 🟢
- Simple Guidance Mechanisms (verified venue: "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2025") defines **D-CFG** (discrete classifier-free guidance), a per-token renormalised geometric blend `p^γ(z_s|z_t,y) = Π_ℓ (1/Z^ℓ)·p_θ(z_s^ℓ|y,z_t)^γ · p_θ(z_s^ℓ|z_t)^(1−γ)` (eqs 7–8), and **D-CBG** (discrete classifier-based guidance), which normalises a noisy classifier only over sequences differing in one position, eq (11), with a first-order Taylor shortcut eq (12). — Schiff et al., §3.1–3.2 [5] 🟢
- Guidance strength vs quality, measured: QM9 ring-count with D-CFG, novel-molecule count falls 276.8 → 209.4 as γ goes 1 → 5 while the property rises 4.54 → 4.88 (Table 31); CIFAR-10 D-CFG FID improves 27.94 (γ=1) → 15.56 (γ=4) then worsens at γ=5 (Table 34). — Schiff et al. [5] 🟢
- Structural warning aimed straight at masked diffusion: "once a token is unmasked at some time t it remains so"; they argue masked models are *less* amenable to guidance than uniform-noise models that can re-edit, and in genomics only their uniform model improved as γ rose (0.91 → 0.94 macro-F1) while both the autoregressive model and MDLM degraded past γ = 2 (Table 4). — Schiff et al., §4, Table 4 [5] 🟢
- DDOM (Denoising Diffusion Optimization Models, ICML 2023) is the primary source for the "condition on y, ask for the max" recipe. (i) Conditioning: a single feed-forward score network ε_θ(x, t, y), with y set to zero 15% of the time to co-train the unconditional model (§3, App. A.2). (ii) **Reweighting toward high y**: partition the offline data into N_B equal-width y-bins and weight the loss by `w_i = |B_i|/(|B_i| + K) · exp(−|ŷ − y_{b_i}|/τ)`, eq (5), used inside the denoising objective eq (6), with K = 0.01·N and τ = 0.1. (iii) At inference `y_test ← max{y | (x,y) ∈ D}` — the dataset maximum, not beyond it — with CFG weight γ = 2.0 in every experiment (Alg. 1 lines 7–15). — Krishnamoorthy, Mashkaria, Grover [9] 🟢
- DDOM's reported effects: reweighting ablation improves 5 of 6 Design-Bench tasks (Ant 0.888 → 0.960, TFBind10 0.644 → 0.688, D'Kitty 0.926 → 0.930, ChEMBL unchanged at 0.633) (Table 2); γ = 0 "performs very poorly" (Fig. 7); on the toy task the best score occurs *at* the dataset maximum and dips past it (Fig. 5); overall mean rank 2.8 of 9 methods, best on Superconductor, but it loses to MINs/COMs on D'Kitty and is last-equal on ChEMBL (Table 1). — Krishnamoorthy et al. [9] 🟢
- DRAKES (ICLR 2025) is the strongest published warning about CFG on a scalar objective with few labels: on protein inverse folding, CFG's success rate is **1.3%** versus 34.4% for the untouched pretrained model and 78.6% for reward fine-tuning — "CFG does not work well for protein sequence design due to limited labeled data" (Table 2). On DNA, where labels are plentiful (~700k sequences), CFG is competitive (activity 5.04, chromatin accessibility 92.1%) but has a worse 3-mer correlation (0.746) than fine-tuning (0.887) (Table 1). Dropping the KL (Kullback–Leibler) term maximises reward and destroys validity (self-consistency RMSD 7.307). — Wang, Uehara et al. [10] 🟢
- NOS (diffusioN Optimized Sampling, NeurIPS 2023) guides in the denoiser's hidden states with a KL penalty, eq (4); its Pareto plots show every guidance method trades sequence log-likelihood for objective value, with the regularisation weight λ as the dominant knob (Fig. 5, Fig. 11). Its most transferable result: under a small edit budget, "the effect size of edit position selection is much larger than that of guidance" — salient *unguided* edits beat guided random edits (Fig. 6). Genetic baselines (AdaLead, PEX) raise the objective fastest and leave the natural-sequence support within 2–4 iterations. — Gruver, Stanton et al. [11] 🟢

**Implication for the repo:** the measured 0.193 at guidance 4 is the literature's own failure mode (strength buys the conditioned property and pays in validity/diversity), and two DDOM ingredients the repo has not run are cheap: bin-reweighting toward high win rate, and conditioning at exactly the observed maximum rather than an extrapolated top bin — while DRAKES says CFG is the weakest arm precisely in the low-label regime the repo is in at 4,850 labelled teams.

## C. Inpainting / fill-in-the-blank

**Plan:** fix some slots of a team, generate the rest.

- MD4: "For conditional generation tasks such as infilling, we find that the simple approach works best — we keep the conditioning tokens unmasked throughout the generation process" (Alg. 2). — Shi et al., §4 [3] 🟢
- MDLM gets this for free from its SUBS parameterisation: "carry-over unmasking" makes unmasked latents copied verbatim by the denoiser and "zero masking probabilities" forbids re-masking, so any token you leave unmasked is a fixed point of the reverse process (eq 7, §3.2.3). — Sahoo et al. [2] 🟢
- LLaDA makes infilling the *training* objective for the instruct model: "we leave the prompt unchanged and mask the tokens in the response independently", loss eq (5) over response tokens only (Fig. 2b). Conditional likelihood uses the fixed-count estimator eq (6), which is stable at 128 Monte Carlo draws versus 1000+ for the naive one. — Nie et al., §2.3–2.4 [4] 🟢
- Quality of infilling vs full generation: MaskGIT's reconstruction study finds an inflection at ~90% masked — holistic content is recovered from ~10% of tokens, and below 90% masking further improvements slow sharply (App. A, Figs. 9–10). Infilling is also where decode order pays most: LLaDA-8B HumanEval-Multiline 16.5% → 25.4% and Split-Line 14.2% → 22.3% purely by changing the unmask order. — Chang et al. [12] 🟢; Kim, Shah et al., Table 4 [13] 🟢

**Implication for the repo:** fixing k of 6 slots and generating the rest requires no retraining and no new loss — just never mask the pinned slots — and is the one conditional mode all four masked-diffusion papers agree on.

## D. Constrained / legality-preserving decoding

**Plan:** mask logits against the feasible set during unmasking instead of learning legality.

- Constrained Discrete Diffusion (NeurIPS 2025; authors Cardei, Christopher, Hartvigsen, Kailkhura, Fioretto — verified) inserts a projection at every denoising step: `x_s = P_C(x′_t) = argmin_y D_KL(x′_t ‖ y) s.t. argmax(y) ∈ C` (eq 5), Kullback–Leibler rather than Euclidean because the iterate lives on the simplex; argmax is relaxed with Gumbel-Softmax (eq 6) and the projection is solved as an augmented-Lagrangian sub-problem. It is training-free and reports zero constraint violations on toxicity thresholds, molecular property constraints and instruction-following. — Cardei et al., §4.1–4.3, §5 [14] 🟢
- Caveat from that same paper: their constraints are measurable functions g_i(x) < τ_i with a violation magnitude ∆g_i. A Showdown validator is a boolean oracle with no gradient and no violation magnitude, so the transferable half of the method is the "project onto the feasible set inside the loop" principle, not the Lagrangian machinery.
- Invalid action masking (journal-ref: FLAIRS Vol. 35, 2022) is the canonical primary source for enforcing hard constraints with a mask: replace invalid logits with a large negative constant M (e.g. −1×10⁸) and renormalise, eqs (2)–(4); Proposition 1 proves the resulting update is a valid policy gradient of the masked policy π′, because masking is a state-dependent differentiable function (identity or constant) applied to the logits. — Huang & Ontañón, §"Invalid Action Masking", Prop. 1 [15] 🟢
- The scaling claim, measured: masking solves the task in ~12% of training steps on every map size 4×4 → 24×24, whereas the invalid-action *penalty* fails to find even the first reward on 10×10 and larger (Table 2). Removing the mask after training degrades but does not destroy behaviour — the model does not fully internalise legality. — Huang & Ontañón, Table 2, Fig. 3 [15] 🟢
- Corroboration from the guidance literature: pushing guidance strength in a discrete generator *reduces* validity (SMILES validity falls with γ, capped at γ = 2 [7]; D-CBG valid-molecule counts fall from 933 to 783 as γ goes 1 → 10 [5] Table 23). Validity is not something guidance improves.

**Implication for the repo:** constrained decoding against the validator is the literature-consistent choice and the three failed attempts to learn legality match Huang & Ontañón's finding that penalties do not scale as the invalid fraction of the action space grows.

## E. Decoding schemes

**Plan:** choose an unmask order and a sampling temperature.

- MaskGIT (CVPR 2022 per the CVF proceedings page; the arXiv record carries no journal-ref) introduced confidence-ordered parallel decoding: predict all masked tokens, sample each, use its predicted probability as a confidence, keep the most confident and re-mask `n = ⌈γ(t/T)·N⌉` of the rest (§3.2). The schedule γ must be continuous and decreasing with γ(0)→1, γ(1)→0 (§3.3). — Chang et al. [12] 🟢
- Cosine wins and wins early: FID/steps at the per-schedule optimum are cosine 6.06 @ T=10, square 6.35 @ 10, cubic 7.26 @ 9, linear 7.51 @ 16, square-root 12.33 @ 32, logarithmic 29.17 @ 60 (Table 3). More iterations are *not* better — every schedule peaks then degrades, hypothesised to be loss of token diversity. — Chang et al., Table 3, Fig. 8 [12] 🟢
- LLaDA's low-confidence remasking (Alg. 5, credited to MaskGIT) is worth an enormous margin over random remasking: BBH 32.1 → 45.0, GSM8K 21.3 → 70.0, Math 9.2 → 30.3, HumanEval 11.6 → 32.9, MBPP 21.0 → 40.2 (Table 9). Their own explanation: it "functions similarly to the annealed sampling method used by default in ARMs, improving accuracy by reducing the diversity of generated sentences". — Nie et al., Table 9, App. B.4 [4] 🟢
- Adaptive order beats random order by a lot where the task has a sequence-dependent natural order: Sudoku 6.88% (random) → 18.51% (top-probability) → 89.49% (top-probability-**margin**); Zebra 76.9% → 98.5% / 98.3%; LLaDA-8B Math 28.5% → 34.3%. The margin criterion (gap between the top two token probabilities) beats raw max-probability whenever two values are near-tied. — Kim, Shah, Kontonis, Kakade, Chen (ICML 2025), Tables 2–4 [13] 🟢
- Order and temperature are partly the same lever. Zheng et al. (ICLR 2025) prove that 32-bit Gumbel-max categorical sampling truncates the Gumbel variable at ≈16.64, which provably amplifies already-large probabilities (Prop. 5.2) and "lowers the effective temperature": switching to 64-bit moves generative perplexity 31.24 → 126.11 and entropy 5.17 → 5.66 (Table 2). Reported masked-diffusion sampling gains can therefore be a hidden temperature effect. — Zheng, Chen, Mao, Liu, Zhu, Zhang, §5.1–5.3 [8] 🟢
- Repo result: decode **order** ≈ null, temperature 0.7 / top-p 0.9 worth +8.7 points (0.260 vs 0.173 baseline), and unconstrained decoding is 0 valid in 80,000.

**Implication for the repo:** the repo's order-null / temperature-positive split is consistent with [4] and [8] reading confidence ordering as a temperature-like diversity cut — the repo has already taken that gain directly, so the only untried order variant with independent published evidence is the **margin** criterion (top-two gap), not top-probability.

## G. Cross-entropy method elite fraction

**Plan:** sweep the elite fraction ρ in the copy-paste loop; ρ = 0.01 currently best.

- "We need to supply the fraction ρ (typically between 0.01 and 0.1) and the parameters N and N₁ in advance." — de Boer, Kroese, Mannor, Rubinstein, §1.3 [28] 🔒 (publisher) / 🟢 (author copy)
- Remark 2.6 (Parameters): "Regarding ρ, it is suggested to take ρ around 0.01, provided n is reasonably large, say n ≥ 100; and it is suggested to take a larger ρ, say ρ ≈ ln(n)/n, for n < 100", with sample size N = c·k for k estimated parameters, c > 1 (5 ≤ c ≤ 10 in their node problems). — [28]
- Remark 2.3 (Smoothed Updating): `v̂_t = α·ŵ_t + (1 − α)·v̂_{t−1}`; "This is especially relevant for optimization problems involving discrete random variables… it prevents the occurrences of 0s and 1s in the parameter vectors; once such an entry is 0 or 1, it often will remain so forever… We found empirically that a value of α between 0.4 ≤ α ≤ 0.9 gives the best results." — [28]
- Their own worked settings bracket the range: max-cut ρ = 0.1, α = 1.0 (no smoothing), N = 1000; travelling-salesman n = 53 nodes ρ = 0.01, α = 0.7, N = 10n² = 28,090. — [28]
- iCEM (CoRL 2020, PMLR v155:1049–1065) does not use a fraction at all: a **fixed** elite count K = 10 with a decaying population `N_i = max(N·γ^(−i), 2K)`, γ = 1.25 — so the effective elite fraction rises every iteration and is floored at 0.5. Other fixed hyperparameters across all tasks: reused-elite fraction ξ = 0.3, momentum α = 0.1, σ_init = 0.5 (Tables S5–S6). — Pinneri et al. [29] 🟢
- iCEM's ablation ranks the additions: colored noise first, then keep-elites / shift-elites and best-action execution, and "the impact of the different additions become more relevant in the low-budget regime" (Fig. 5, Fig. S9). Elites are carried both across inner iterations and across time steps, never discarded. — [29] 🟢
- Repo result: ρ 0.20 → 0.01 monotone, copy-paste win rate 0.479 → 0.540, past a real team's 0.470, novelty intact.

**Implication for the repo:** ρ = 0.01 is exactly the tutorial's recommended value for large problems, so the sweep has landed on the literature's default rather than past it — the untried levers with primary-source backing are α-smoothing at 0.4–0.9 (the repo's copy-paste has none, and the tutorial says smoothing matters *specifically* for discrete variables) and iCEM's fixed-K-with-decaying-population, which floors the elite fraction instead of shrinking it.
